Biologically unavoidable sequences: a classification and related notes

10 September 2026 — research packet for discussion

The main claim

Under Definition 1 of Alexander’s Biologically unavoidable sequences [1], the biologically unavoidable sequences over a finite alphabet are exactly the eventually periodic sequences. The new direction is an explicit avoiding population for every other sequence. The eventually periodic direction is Theorem 10 of the published paper (Theorem 6 of the nine-page arXiv version), with the small proof repair below.

This packet presents a proof for scrutiny. The negative construction has substantial Lean verification, described in the last section. Neither independent human review nor literature priority is claimed.

We use zero-based indices and edge labels. A population is an infinite directed graph with finitely many roots, finite outdegree at every vertex, real birthdates strictly increasing along edges and finite sublevel sets, and an incoming edge of each alphabet label at every non-root. A realizing path may start at any vertex.

1. The binary construction and proof

Fix s:ℕ→{0,1}s:\mathbb N\to\{0,1\} and put

r(2j)=s(j),r(2j+1)=1−s(j).r(2j)=s(j),\qquad r(2j+1)=1-s(j).

Let PsP_s have vertices ℕ\mathbb N and birthdate t(v)=vt(v)=v. For every v≥2v\ge2 put the following two incoming edges, and no others:

v−1→vwith label r(v),v-1\longrightarrow v\quad\text{with label }r(v), v−2→vwith label 1−r(v).v-2\longrightarrow v\quad\text{with label }1-r(v).

There is no edge 0→10\to1. The roots are exactly 0 and 1. Vertex 0 has one child, and each vertex u≥1u\ge1 has two children. Every non-root has two distinct parents, with complementary labels. Birthdates increase strictly, their sublevel sets are finite, and the graph is infinite. Thus these are populations in the paper’s precise edge-labelled sense.

Claim. If PsP_s realizes ss, then ss is eventually periodic.

Suppose v0,v1,…v_0,v_1,\ldots is an infinite matching path. Each increment is 1 or 2. Inductively,

vk≥2k.v_k\ge 2k.

Indeed, if vk≥2k+1v_k\ge2k+1, the next vertex is at least 2k+22k+2. If vk=2kv_k=2k, an increment of 1 would have label r(2k+1)=1−s(k)r(2k+1)=1-s(k), so is impossible. At k=0k=0 this edge is absent; the same conclusion holds. The base case is v0≥0v_0\ge0.

Consequently dk=vk−2kd_k=v_k-2k is a nonnegative integer sequence with increments in {−1,0}\{-1,0\}. It eventually stabilizes at some d≥0d\ge0. From some KK onward every path increment is therefore 2, so

s(k)=1−r(2k+d+2),k≥K.s(k)=1-r(2k+d+2),\qquad k\ge K.

If d=2e+1d=2e+1, this says s(k)=s(k+e+1)s(k)=s(k+e+1), giving positive eventual period e+1e+1. If d=2ed=2e, it says s(k)=1−s(k+e+1)s(k)=1-s(k+e+1); applying the relation twice gives period 2e+22e+2. Either case forces eventual periodicity.

Thus every non-eventually-periodic binary target is avoided by its own PsP_s, from all starting vertices. The graph depends on the target; this does not produce one graph avoiding every aperiodic sequence.

2. Finite alphabets and vertex genders

Let AA be finite and s:ℕ→As:\mathbb N\to A not eventually periodic. At least one indicator projection π:A→{0,1}\pi:A\to\{0,1\} has a non-eventually-periodic image π∘s\pi\circ s. Otherwise all finitely many symbol indicators would be eventually periodic. Beyond the maximum of their starting thresholds, a common multiple of their positive periods would be a period of ss.

Construct the binary population Pπ∘sP_{\pi\circ s}. Replace each vertex vv by the copies (v,a)(v,a), a∈Aa\in A. For every binary edge u→vu\to v with label cc, add

(u,a)→(v,b)with label afor all a,b∈A satisfying π(a)=c. (u,a)\longrightarrow(v,b)\quad\text{with label }a \quad\text{for all }a,b\in A\text{ satisfying }\pi(a)=c.

Give each copy birthdate vv. There are 2|A|2|A| roots, at most 2|A|2|A| children per vertex, and exactly one incoming parent of each label at every non-root. The other axioms are inherited. An ss-path would project to a π∘s\pi\circ s-path in the binary population, which is impossible.

Moreover, label aa is the source copy’s second coordinate: assigning gender aa to (v,a)(v,a) makes this a vertex-gendered population. For a binary alphabet this yields four roots and at most four children per vertex. The smaller two-root/two-child construction uses the more general edge-labelled definition.

Thue–Morse example. Put t(k)t(k) equal to the parity of the number of ones in the binary expansion of kk. Then t(2k)=t(k)t(2k)=t(k) and t(2k+1)=1−t(k)t(2k+1)=1-t(k), so the auxiliary sequence rr equals tt itself. The incoming labels at vv are simply t(v)t(v) and 1−t(v)1-t(v).

For completeness, tt is not eventually periodic. If p>0p>0 were an eventual period, then for every sufficiently large mm, t(2m−p)=t(2m)=1t(2^m-p)=t(2^m)=1. But 2m−p2^m-p is the mm-bit complement of p−1p-1, so

t(2m−p)=(mmod⁡2)xort(p−1),t(2^m-p)=(m\bmod2)\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}t(p-1),

which alternates with mm. This is a contradiction.

3. A small repair to the positive direction

Write VjV_j for the finite set of vertices reachable within jj edges of a root. In Theorem 10, deleting Vk−1V_{k-1} can leave a surviving non-root with only some of its required incoming labels.

For example, take vertices ai,bia_i,b_i at integer time i≥0i\ge0, with the time-zero pair as roots. Normally every vertex at time i≥1i\ge1 has parent ai−1a_{i-1} of label 0 and parent bi−1b_{i-1} of label 1. Replace the label-0 parent of a2a_2 by a0a_0. Deleting the roots leaves a2a_2 with a label-1 parent but no label-0 parent.

After deleting Vk−1V_{k-1}, also delete all remaining incoming edges to the surviving boundary Vk∖Vk−1V_k\backslash V_{k-1}. Those finitely many vertices become roots. Every vertex outside VkV_k keeps all its original parents: a parent in Vk−1V_{k-1} would put its child in VkV_k. Hence the modified graph is a population. Apply the periodic-path theorem there, and prepend the prescribed kk labels in the original graph. Its starting point lies outside Vk−1V_{k-1}, so these backward choices cannot reach a root too early. The purely periodic case needs no deletion.

This repairs the proof step and preserves the positive theorem. More generally, prepending any finite word preserves biological unavoidability in both directions.

4. The other questions in the paper

Universal avoiding populations: a scoped negative answer

Fix an avoidable sequence ss. Interpret an embedding as an injective vertex map preserving directed edges and their labels. Then there is no universal population avoiding ss. In fact, even a countable family of countable finite-outdegree hosts cannot contain every avoiding population under these embeddings.

Here is the argument, using the standard degree-growth diagonal obstruction for locally finite graphs [2]. Fix one avoiding population PP. Replace each vertex vv by a nonempty finite number f(v)f(v) of copies, and replace each edge by all corresponding edges between its copy sets. Birthdates are inherited. All axioms survive, and projection and lifting show that this blowup has exactly the same infinite label language as PP.

Choose a directed ray v0→v1→⋯v_0\to v_1\to\cdots in PP; one exists by finite roots, finite outdegree, and König’s lemma. Enumerate all pairs of a proposed host and one of its vertices as (Wi,ui)(W_i,u_i). Let MiM_i be the maximum outdegree in the finite forward ball of radius ii about uiu_i. Set f(vi+1)=Mi+1f(v_{i+1})=M_i+1, and take one copy elsewhere, including at v0v_0. If an embedding sent v0v_0 to the enumerated uiu_i, a chosen copy of viv_i would map inside that ball yet have at least Mi+1M_i+1 distinct children. Injectivity contradicts the definition of MiM_i.

The hosts need not avoid ss. The result also excludes the more restrictive induced or birthdate-preserving embeddings. It says nothing comparable about noninjective homomorphisms, subdivisions, or classes with a fixed source-degree bound. The paper’s broader wording does not select one of these conventions, so this is a scoped answer.

Matching-tree ranks: exact barriers, but a coarse ordinal invariant

For a start vv, let T(v,s)T(v,s) be the tree of finite paths matching prefixes of ss. It is finitely branching. König’s lemma gives

s is not realized from v⇔T(v,s) has finite height.s\text{ is not realized from }v \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T(v,s)\text{ has finite height}.

Thus PP avoids ss exactly when each starting vertex has some finite blocking prefix. The bound may depend on the start.

Every finite word occurs somewhere in every population: choose an endpoint outside Vm−1V_{m-1} for a word of length mm, and select parents in reverse label order. Therefore, in an avoiding population, the finite heights are unbounded over all starts. If a super-root is attached above all the T(v,s)T(v,s), its well-founded rank is exactly ω\omega. This gives a precise rank test, but that one aggregate rank cannot distinguish avoiders. It is not a full classification by richer ordinal invariants.

A category consequence

Let KvK_v be the set of sequences realized from vv. Finite branching makes KvK_v closed: if every finite prefix is realizable from vv, König’s lemma gives the infinite path. The population is countable by its finite birthdate sublevels, so L(P)=⋃vKvL(P)=\bigcup_vK_v is FσF_\sigma.

If some KvK_v contains a cylinder of all sequences extending a finite word ww, deleting the ww part of each realizing path shows that PP realizes every sequence. Thus either PP is universal, or every KvK_v is nowhere dense, L(P)L(P) is meagre, and the avoided language is a dense GδG_\delta.

5. Why individual avoidance is compatible with many aperiodic paths

Every population has a root-starting path with labels in any prescribed nonempty one-sided subshift XX (a closed, forward-shift-invariant set).

Choose s∈Xs\in X. For arbitrarily large NN, choose an endpoint outside VN−1V_{N-1}. Its ancestor set is finite, so take a prefix of ss longer than any path ending there. Prescribe that prefix backwards until a root is reached. Reading forward gives a factor of ss, on a root path of length at least NN. The forest of such factor-labelled root paths is prefix closed, finitely branching and has finitely many roots. König’s lemma gives a path whose every prefix is a factor of ss, hence whose labels lie in XX.

Apply this to the orbit closure of a mechanical word sα(k)=⌊(k+1)α⌋−⌊kα⌋s_\alpha(k)=\lfloor(k+1)\alpha\rfloor-\lfloor k\alpha\rfloor. Every block of length mm has a number of ones differing from mαm\alpha by less than one. The root path in that orbit closure therefore has frequency α\alpha. For irrational α\alpha it is aperiodic, and different slopes give different sequences. Every binary population realizes continuum many aperiodic sequences, despite every individual aperiodic sequence having an avoiding population.

6. Universality and finite observation in the new family

The construction has a second exact property:

Ps realizes every binary sequence⇔s is eventually periodic.P_s\text{ realizes every binary sequence} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s\text{ is eventually periodic}.

The negative direction is §1. If ss has eventual period pp, then rr has eventual period q=2pq=2p. Form a finite residue multigraph modulo qq, with the two incoming transitions into residue aa, labelled by the eventual r(a)r(a) and its complement, and marked with delays 1 and 2. Every state has an incoming transition of each label. Every finite word can therefore be read backwards; finite branching and finitely many possible starts give an infinite path for every infinite word. Starting sufficiently late in PsP_s, lift each transition by its marked delay. This realizes the word in the actual population. The marked delays retain the information needed to lift the quotient path.

The graph through vertex NN depends only on s(0),…,s(⌊N/2⌋)s(0),\ldots,s(\lfloor N/2\rfloor). Any such prefix can be continued with zeros or with Thue–Morse. The resulting populations agree on that entire finite region, but one is universal and the other is not. This also obstructs any finite adaptive sequence of local edge queries.

Even a program describing the graph does not decide universality. Given a machine MM, let sM(k)s_M(k) be the kkth Thue–Morse bit if MM has not halted within kk steps, and zero otherwise. This sequence and its graph are total computable objects. The graph is universal exactly when MM halts. Conversely, using zeros until a halt is detected and then starting a Thue–Morse tail gives a universal graph exactly when MM never halts. Thus neither universality nor non-universality is semidecidable on these presentations, even with the promise that all population axioms hold.

For a fixed aperiodic ss, breadth-first matching from any vertex terminates with an empty set by §1 and König’s lemma. There is no bound uniform in the target even for starting vertex 1: a target with N+1N+1 initial zeros and an aperiodic tail admits NN matching edges along vk=1+2kv_k=1+2k. Obtaining useful explicit bounds for the fixed Thue–Morse target was left as a quantitative question in the September 10 packet. The countable-avoidance addendum also proves that any prescribed countable family of aperiodic targets can be avoided simultaneously, with optimal uniform outdegree for every finite alphabet.
