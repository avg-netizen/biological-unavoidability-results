Simultaneous complement avoidance and explicit Thue–Morse barriers

20 September 2026 — continuation for discussion

This continuation resolves two questions left in the earlier packet: simultaneous avoidance of the two complementary Thue–Morse sequences, and an explicit finite matching bound for the particular Thue–Morse population. The first result holds for every non-eventually-periodic binary sequence. These are hand proofs with finite computational controls; independent review and priority checking remain. The earlier Lean files do not certify these additions.

1. One population avoids both complementary targets

For a binary sequence ss, write s‾(k)=1−s(k)\bar s(k)=1-s(k) and put

r(2j)=s(j),r(2j+1)=1−s(j).r(2j)=s(j),\qquad r(2j+1)=1-s(j).

The population PsP_s has vertices ℕ\mathbb N, birthdate vv at vertex vv, and, for every v≥2v\ge2, precisely the incoming edges

v−1→v labelled r(v),v−2→v labelled 1−r(v).v-1\longrightarrow v\text{ labelled }r(v),\qquad v-2\longrightarrow v\text{ labelled }1-r(v).

There is no edge 0→10\to1. The roots are 0 and 1; every non-root has a parent of each label; every vertex has at most two children. All birthdate sublevel sets are finite. Thus this is an Alexander population in the edge-labelled convention, with no restriction on a realizing path’s start.

Theorem. If ss is not eventually periodic, PsP_s realizes neither ss nor s‾\bar s.

The original packet proves avoidance of ss. Here is a self-contained joint argument. Let v0,v1,…v_0,v_1,\ldots be a hypothetical matching path and put dk=vk−2kd_k=v_k-2k. Every increment of dkd_k is −1-1 or 0.

For target ss, induction gives vk≥2kv_k\ge2k: at vk=2kv_k=2k, the increment-one edge, if present, has label r(2k+1)=1−s(k)r(2k+1)=1-s(k) and cannot match. Hence dk≥0d_k\ge0 and eventually dk=dd_k=d is constant.

For target s‾\bar s, the corresponding induction gives vk≥2k−1v_k\ge2k-1. If vk>2k−1v_k>2k-1, any edge preserves the next lower bound. At equality, an increment-one edge has label r(2k)=s(k)r(2k)=s(k) and cannot match. Thus dk≥−1d_k\ge-1 and again it stabilizes. In fact an infinite matching path cannot visit dk=−1d_k=-1: both outgoing edges there have label s(k)s(k), since the increment-two label is 1−r(2k+1)=s(k)1-r(2k+1)=s(k). This also handles that exceptional offset without invoking a shift of length zero.

On the constant-offset tail, every increment is two. For d≥0d\ge0, put q=⌊d/2⌋+1>0q=\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1>0. The matching equation is as follows:

For target ss: even dd gives s(k)=1−s(k+q)s(k)=1-s(k+q), and odd dd gives s(k)=s(k+q)s(k)=s(k+q). For target s‾\bar s: even dd gives s(k)=s(k+q)s(k)=s(k+q), and odd dd gives s(k)=1−s(k+q)s(k)=1-s(k+q).

Either equality makes ss eventually periodic, with period qq or 2q2q. This proves the theorem. The boundary −1-1, rather than 0, is the only new ingredient needed for the complementary target.

For a periodic control of period pp, the construction correctly fails to avoid either target: the increment-two paths starting at 2p−12p-1 and 2p−22p-2 realize ss and s‾\bar s, respectively. These controls include p=1p=1 and the actual root convention.

Therefore Q(Ps)Q(P_s) avoids μ(s)\mu(s) and μ(s‾)\mu(\bar s) whenever ss is aperiodic. For Thue–Morse tt, both tt and t‾\bar t are fixed by μ\mu, so PtP_t and every iterate Qn(Pt)Q^n(P_t) avoid both. 2. A short-run lemma for Thue–Morse

Write t(k)=popcount⁡(k)mod⁡2t(k)=\operatorname{popcount}(k)\bmod2, and, for q≥1q\ge1, define

cq(k)=t(k)xort(k+q),λ(q)=2ν2(q).c_q(k)=t(k)\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}t(k+q),\qquad \lambda(q)=2^{\nu_2(q)}.

Lemma. Every constant consecutive run of cqc_q has length at most 3λ(q)3\lambda(q), independently of where the run starts.

The identities t(2n)=t(n)t(2n)=t(n) and t(2n+1)=1−t(n)t(2n+1)=1-t(n) imply that tt has no three consecutive equal bits: any three positions include an even/odd pair (2n,2n+1)(2n,2n+1).

First let q=2r+1q=2r+1 be odd. Then

cq(2n)=1xort(n)xort(n+r),c_q(2n)=1\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}t(n)\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}t(n+r), cq(2n+1)=1xort(n)xort(n+r+1).c_q(2n+1)=1\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}t(n)\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}t(n+r+1).

Four equal values starting at 2n2n would force t(n+r)=t(n+r+1)=t(n+r+2)t(n+r)=t(n+r+1)=t(n+r+2), by comparing the two aligned pairs. Four equal values starting at 2n+12n+1 would instead force t(n)=t(n+1)=t(n+2)t(n)=t(n+1)=t(n+2), by comparing the pairs (2n+1,2n+2)(2n+1,2n+2) and (2n+3,2n+4)(2n+3,2n+4). Both are impossible. Thus the bound is three for odd qq.

Finally c2q(2n)=c2q(2n+1)=cq(n)c_{2q}(2n)=c_{2q}(2n+1)=c_q(n). Each doubling of the shift doubles each constant run. Removing all powers of two proves the lemma. This elementary symbolic-sequence lemma is used here as a tool; no claim that the lemma itself is new is intended.

3. An explicit bound for every starting vertex

For the Thue–Morse population PtP_t, r=tr=t. Define

W(i)=∑d=0iλ(⌊d/2⌋+1).W(i)=\sum_{d=0}^{i}\lambda\!\left(\left\lfloor d/2\right\rfloor+1\right).

Theorem. A path starting at vertex ii and matching a prefix of tt has at most i+3W(i)i+3W(i) edges. A path matching a prefix of t‾\bar t has at most i+1+3W(i)i+1+3W(i) edges. In particular neither target can match

B(i)=i+2+3W(i)B(i)=i+2+3W(i)

edges from that vertex. This is a sufficient barrier, not a claimed optimal one.

To prove it, separate increment-one edges from maximal consecutive runs of increment-two edges. Each increment-one edge decreases dkd_k by one; there are at most ii such edges for target tt and at most i+1i+1 for target t‾\bar t. Each nonnegative offset dd is visited in at most one plateau. On its increment-two run, the equations above make cq(k)c_q(k) constant, where q=⌊d/2⌋+1q=\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1. The lemma bounds this run by 3λ(q)3\lambda(q). Summing over 0≤d≤i0\le d\le i proves the stated bounds. Offset −1-1 contributes no edges for the complementary target.

The bound grows as O((i+1)log⁡(i+2))O((i+1)\log(i+2)). For an explicit estimate put m=⌊i/2⌋+1m=\lfloor i/2\rfloor+1 and

S(m)=∑q=1mλ(q)=m+∑j≥12j−1⌊m/2j⌋≤m(1+12⌊log⁡2m⌋).S(m)=\sum_{q=1}^{m}\lambda(q) =m+\sum_{j\ge1}2^{j-1}\left\lfloor m/2^j\right\rfloor \le m\left(1+\tfrac12\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor\right).

Since W(i)≤2S(m)W(i)\le2S(m),

B(i)≤i+2+6m+3m⌊log⁡2m⌋.B(i)\le i+2+6m+3m\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor.

For exact integer evaluation, S(0)=0S(0)=0 and S(m)=⌈m/2⌉+2S(⌊m/2⌋)S(m)=\lceil m/2\rceil+2S(\lfloor m/2\rfloor). Also W(2h+1)=2S(h+1)W(2h+1)=2S(h+1) and W(2h)=2S(h)+λ(h+1)W(2h)=2S(h)+\lambda(h+1).

This replaces a qualitative termination argument for a fixed target with a computable a priori cutoff. It does not contradict the earlier absence of a target-uniform bound at fixed start: an arbitrary aperiodic target may begin with an arbitrarily long constant block.

4. Checks, limitations and remaining questions

The accompanying evidence/44s-complement-barrier-checks.py enumerates finite paths directly from the graph’s two incoming-edge formulas, checks both boundary invariants on all short binary targets, uses periodic positive controls, and compares actual Thue–Morse first-empty times with B(i)B(i). It also checks the short-run lemma over finite ranges. The JSON records the precise ranges and outcomes; finite checks are not the infinite proofs.

The logarithmic factor may be avoidable: the proof separately charges every offset’s longest possible run even when those runs cannot be concatenated along one matching path. A sharp asymptotic or exact barrier remains open locally. Other embedding/homomorphism versions of universal avoiding populations and more informative ordinal invariants remain outside these results.
