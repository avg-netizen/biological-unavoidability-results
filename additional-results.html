Additional structural and cellular-automaton observations

10 September 2026 — optional supplement

These hand arguments supplement the main author note. They are not part of the supplied Lean certificate, and no literature priority is claimed. The first two use Alexander’s population axioms as stated in the main note.

1. Bounded root-path spread forces universality

Let d(v)d(v) and D(v)D(v) be the shortest and longest root-to-vv path lengths. Both exist because the ancestor set is finite. Put Δ(v)=D(v)−d(v)\Delta(v)=D(v)-d(v).

Proposition. If Δ(v)≤K\Delta(v)\le K at infinitely many vertices, for some fixed finite KK, then every label sequence is realized. More precisely, each sequence is realized from some vertex within KK edges of a root.

Fix a desired sequence ss and prepend any word of length KK, obtaining aa. At a vertex vv with Δ(v)≤K\Delta(v)\le K, prescribe the first D(v)D(v) symbols of aa backwards until a root is reached. If the resulting path has length ℓ\ell, then

d(v)≤ℓ≤D(v),j=D(v)−ℓ∈{0,…,K}.d(v)\le\ell\le D(v),\qquad j=D(v)-\ell\in\{0,\ldots,K\}.

Read forwards, its labels are a[j:D(v)]a[j:D(v)]. Delete the first K−jK-j edges. The remaining path starts within KK edges of a root and spells the first D(v)−KD(v)-K symbols of ss, whenever D(v)≥KD(v)\ge K. The infinitely many chosen vertices have unbounded d(v)d(v), because only finitely many vertices are reachable within any fixed number of steps from the finitely many roots. Thus arbitrarily long prefixes of ss occur from the finite set VKV_K. König’s lemma supplies an infinite realization.

Consequently every non-universal population has only finitely many vertices with Δ(v)≤K\Delta(v)\le K, for each finite KK. In particular, a population whose edges all advance one synchronous generation realizes every sequence; such a search restriction cannot produce an avoider. The construction in the main note uses edge lengths 1 and 2.

2. Minimum uniform branching and finite degree defects

For an nn-letter alphabet, a population cannot have outdegree at most n−1n-1 at every vertex. If FF is finite, ancestrally closed, contains all rr roots, and has NN vertices, then

n(N−r)≤|E(F)|≤(n−1)N,n(N-r)\le |E(F)|\le(n-1)N,

forcing N≤nrN\le nr. Such sets exhaust the infinite graph, a contradiction. For example take increasing birthdate sublevels containing all roots.

If instead every outdegree is at most nn, set o(v)=n−outdeg⁡(v)o(v)=n-\operatorname{outdeg}(v) and i(v)=indeg⁡(v)−ni(v)=\operatorname{indeg}(v)-n for non-roots. Both are nonnegative. Exact counting on the same ancestral sets gives

|E(F,V∖F)|=nr−∑v∈Fo(v)−∑v∈Fv not a rooti(v).|E(F,V\backslash F)|=nr-\sum_{v\in F}o(v) -\sum_{\substack{v\in F\\v\text{ not a root}}}i(v).

Hence the total integer degree defect is at most nrnr. Outside finitely many exceptional vertices, indegree and outdegree are exactly nn, with one incoming edge of each label. Every such ancestral cut has at most nrnr crossing edges. The binary avoiding construction lies at this minimum uniform branching threshold; degree two does not force universality.

3. A directional CA bound under incomplete rule information

Let DD be a finite set of spatial displacements from a parent cell to a child. Assume a newborn has at least b≥1b\ge1 distinct live predecessors among those displacements, and a survivor remains at its own position. For a nonempty finite live configuration, put

Ha(t)=max⁡{a⋅z:z is live at time t},H_a(t)=\max\{a\cdot z:z\text{ is live at time }t\},

where aa is any linear spatial direction, and let

κb(a)=max⁡(0,the bth largest of {a⋅d:d∈D}).\kappa_b(a)=\max\bigl(0,\text{the }b\text{th largest of } \{a\cdot d:d\in D\}\bigr).

Proposition. Ha(t+1)≤Ha(t)+κb(a)H_a(t+1)\le H_a(t)+\kappa_b(a) whenever the next configuration is nonempty, hence Ha(t)≤Ha(0)+tκb(a)H_a(t)\le H_a(0)+t\kappa_b(a) while live cells remain.

Among any bb live predecessor displacements, at least one has projection at most the bbth largest projection in all of DD. For a birth at zz, choose that predecessor z−dz-d. Then

a⋅z=a⋅(z−d)+a⋅d≤Ha(t)+κb(a).a\cdot z=a\cdot(z-d)+a\cdot d\le H_a(t)+\kappa_b(a).

Survivors satisfy the same inequality because κb(a)≥0\kappa_b(a)\ge0. An empty configuration cannot produce births under b≥1b\ge1, so the undefined maximum on an empty set causes no need for a new case of the bound.

This supplies a spatial envelope without specifying the entire local rule. It does not predict the future configuration or assert existence of a spaceship achieving the envelope. It is closely related to the established CA speed-limit arguments of Nathaniel Johnston, The B36/S125 “2x2” Life-Like Cellular Automaton, arXiv:1203.1644, Section 3, and Alexander’s application. It is included as a reusable formulation under partial rule information, not advertised as a new sharp speed-limit theorem.
